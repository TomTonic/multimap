# Review of the approach (2026-10-07): small, aimed experiments before a decision

Words as in [GLOSSARY.md](GLOSSARY.md). Why: after gate 5 (m47) the one page is complete, and the user wants a consistent point
from which to judge the whole approach against the earlier ones (`main`, `node-layout` `7b8a8d8`, step 3.5 `fe120f5`), instead of
tuning on in a local optimum. Step 6 as planned (N5 out, no tails, range nodes) rests until this review is done.

## What we know (m47 and the probes of 2026-10-07)

- Small maps (4,096 and 16,384 keys) lost speed with the multi-key pages: churn and build against the competitor at 4,096 keys,
  step 3.5 → now: `uint64` street real 1.79 / 1.82 → 1.18 / 1.22, strings street real 1.59 / 1.62 → 0.89 / 0.97, `uint64` street
  single-value 1.07 / 1.09 → 0.75 / 0.83, strings dirs single-value 0.88 / 0.84 → 0.64 / 0.59. Large maps gained (street single-value
  lookups 1.72 → 2.13, churn 0.98 → 1.17). Memory fell by about 40 % (street real 90 → 54 B a key, single-value 67 → 27).
  At 4K and 16K `main` was 2 to 8 % ahead of `node-layout` (consolidation-vs-main, 2026-09-30), `node-layout` 1 to 15 % ahead of 3.5.
- A lookup of a key in a small single-value tree (street, 4,096): about 37 % the search in the page (`locate`, linear over the
  remainders), about 37 % the descent through the byte nodes (3.2 on average), 17 % more for strings (the copy of the value).
- Shape of the routing part at full size: byte nodes are 4 to 10 % of the heap; nodes on the way to a key: street 5.9, dirs **11.9**,
  `u64` 2.1.

## The user's answers that bind the review

1. R1 and R2 (objects of 128-byte multiples) are a means: speed, and limiting the fragmentation of long-running processes.
2. A hybrid only if both ends work alone and the border can be moved from one end to the other.
3. The fragmentation that matters: a database process that runs for weeks and fails at some allocation because the heap is full of
   small objects with gaps (Go does not move objects).
4. Path-like keys (directories, URLs) will be a main case.
5. Widen the benchmark base with the key kinds the bench has (`str`, `uuid`, `email`, `url`, `path`), but with small experiments
   first, not a run of 14 hours.

## E1: one key per page (prediction, before the code)

The variant is the hybrid's border at 1: every key its own page (the one-key form), the routing part above it as today. Changes on the
branch only: a different key that meets a single-key page puts a node above it (no `pair`), no merge after a removal, and no
multi-key page is ever built. Nothing else changes (the page, the head, the value overflow, the reads of 5.5e).

**Prediction** (bench on WSL, 4,096 keys, against `baseline` = step 3.5):
- churn and build: **0.90 to 1.10 of step 3.5** (the multi-key pages are what cost the 30 to 45 %); against today 25 to 45 % faster;
- `valuesFor`: 0.95 to 1.10 of step 3.5 (the one-key page reads like 3.5's single-key page, the descent is a level or two deeper than
  today's: one more node, no linear search in the page);
- memory (probe, block bytes a key): single-value 45 to 65 B a key (3.5: 67 heap; today 27), real mix 75 to 95 (3.5: 90; today 54):
  one object a key of 32 or 64 bytes and more byte nodes.

If churn and build stay well below 3.5 (below 0.85), the loss is not the multi-key page but something the one page brought to every
page (head, layout, value handling), and the hybrid would not help.

## E2: where the writes go (prediction)

In today's small single-value trees the page's `Add` and `Remove` (shifting the key lengths, remainders and values, and `locate`) take
the largest share of churn, followed by the descent and the bursts and merges; in E1 the page work is a few nanoseconds and the descent
and allocation of new single-key pages (one allocation for every new key) take the most.

## E6: hysteresis between burst and merge (prediction, before the code)

**Today:** a page bursts when it is full (more than 512 bytes, or a limit); after a removal that leaves a page with at most two entries,
the node above is merged into one page if all its children fit **512 bytes** together (`mergeFits`). A merged page is therefore often
nearly full and bursts again at the next few adds: in the churn of street single-value at 4,096 keys 8.4 bursts and 8.3 merges per 1,000
operations, of 24 to 27 entries each.

**The rule** (branch `exp-hysteresis`, one constant): a merge happens only if the merged page needs **at most half** of the largest page
(256 bytes), the same 50 % that brings a value overflow back into a page (`BackFits`). A merged page then has room for as many entries
again before it bursts. Nothing else changes (the burst, the pairing of two keys, the one-key form of a page left with one key).

**Prediction** (probe, WSL, and the bench on the PC as in E1, against today and step 3.5):
- events, steady-state cycle of street single-value 4,096: merges done 8.3 → **at most 2** per 1,000, bursts 8.4 → **at most 2**;
  merges tried about as before (the trigger is the same);
- churn of single-value at 4,096 keys **15 to 25 % less time** (street, dirs, `u64`), at 16,384 keys 5 to 15 %; churn of the natural mix
  5 to 15 %; build (the build stream has removals too) 0 to 10 %; `valuesFor` ±3 %;
- against `btree-map`, street single-value churn 0.75 → 0.88 to 0.95;
- memory: the tree as built from the corpus is the same (no removal); after removing every second key +1 to +3 B a key (fewer merges).

## E3: fragmentation over a long run (prediction, before the run)

**Method:** `bench/cmd/bench/fragprobe_test.go` (`MKFRAG=1`): one candidate per process, the build stream, then 200 churn cycles
(each inserts and deletes the corpus's values about once again, with long-lived values taken out and put back); after the build and
every 20 cycles a GC and `runtime/metrics`: bytes of live objects, bytes unused in the spans that hold them (the fragmentation: Go gives a
span back only when all its objects are dead), free pages kept, pages released, number of objects. Candidates: today, hysteresis (E6),
one key per page (E1), step 3.5 (`baseline`), `btree-sets` / `btree-map`. Corpora: street natural (212,449 keys), street single-value,
`u64` natural (262,144). WSL (Linux, as a database server would run).

**Prediction:**
- no candidate's heap grows without bound: from cycle 20 to cycle 200 the total heap (live + unused + free) changes by less than 10 %;
  Go reuses the free slots of a size class, so the unused bytes are bounded by the peak of each class;
- unused / live after 200 cycles: the page variants 0.05 to 0.25 (objects of 32 to 512 bytes that change their class on growth and
  shrink), hysteresis below today (fewer bursts and merges, so fewer class changes); one key per page and step 3.5 0.05 to 0.15 (many
  objects of 32 and 64 bytes, few class changes); `btree` below 0.10;
- the total heap after 200 cycles, in bytes a key: the page variants stay the smallest (the 40 % less memory holds over the run).
